LoRA freezes a weight matrix and learns a correction that is the product of two thin matrices. The rank of that product is the only real knob, and it is tempting to turn it up. This placeholder post sketches what turning it does, with synthetic numbers shaped like a real sweep.

The update as pixels

For a frozen weight W0∈Rd×kW_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}W0​∈Rd×k, LoRA trains

W=W0+αrBA,B∈Rd×r,A∈Rr×k,r≪min⁡(d,k).\begin{gathered} W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A, \\ B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},\quad A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k},\quad r \ll \min(d, k). \end{gathered} W=W0​+rα​BA,B∈Rd×r,A∈Rr×k,r≪min(d,k).​

BBB starts at zero, so training begins exactly at the pretrained model.1 Drag the rank and watch the product gain structure.

Learning rate against rank

The useful question is not which rank wins but whether the best learning rate moves with it. With the α/r\alpha / rα/r scaling, it mostly does not.2 Hover the chart to read values; use the chips to hide or isolate a series.

Training curves

Raw loss is noisy. An exponential moving average with factor β\betaβ shows the trend:

ℓˉt=β ℓˉt−1+(1−β) ℓt.\bar{\ell}_t = \beta\, \bar{\ell}_{t-1} + (1 - \beta)\, \ell_t . ℓˉt​=βℓˉt−1​+(1−β)ℓt​.
